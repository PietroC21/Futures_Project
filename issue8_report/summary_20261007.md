# Issue #8 — MBO analysis of stale-quote resolution

## 1. Objective and scope

This is an order-level robustness check of the main MBP-1 study. The question is what happens to resting ES 0DTE option orders after Issue #6 flags the displayed quote as economically stale: are the orders cancelled, filled, modified, or still unresolved when the observation window ends?

We use a deliberately small historical MBO sample. The goal is to characterize **observed order activity**, not to infer the identities or intentions of liquidity providers or to simulate an executable trading strategy.

| Item | Sample |
|---|---|
| Trading date | October 7, 2026 |
| Analysis window | 10:00–10:30 a.m. Eastern |
| Instruments with stale pairs | 18 ES 0DTE option contracts |
| Input | Issue #6 stale pairs, Issue #4 futures events, and Databento `GLBX.MDP3` option `mbo` |
| Stale event–option pairs analyzed | **828** |
| Futures move groups | 764 single-tick; 64 multi-tick |
| MBO-absent pairs after completing instrument coverage | **0** |

The MBO contracts were obtained in separate batches to avoid redownloading the initial four contracts. The final analysis includes all 18 option instruments represented in the selected stale-pair sample. This is a **30-minute, single-day extension**, not a five-day estimate.

## 2. Method

We take Issue #6 observations with `status == "stale"`. Each observation provides an option instrument, the stale bid or ask, the futures-event start time (`t0`), and the observation cutoff. Issue #8 reconstructs the relevant resting order state from MBO messages and matches orders on the stale side at the stale price before `t0`. Order actions are examined up to the earlier of quote resolution and the event observation cutoff.

The analysis distinguishes additions (`A`), cancellations/removals (`C`), resting-order fills (`F`), modifications (`M`), and book resets (`R`). Aggressor trade (`T`) messages are not independently counted as resting-order fills. Paired `F`/`C` messages are handled to avoid counting the same executed quantity as both a fill and a cancellation. The results preserve order IDs and action records for inspection.

Each stale event–option pair receives one mutually exclusive label:

- **Cancelled:** observed cancellation/removal is the classified outcome.
- **Filled:** observed execution is the classified outcome.
- **Modified:** observed modification is the classified outcome.
- **Mixed:** more than one type of order action is observed.
- **Unclassified:** a definitive quote-level outcome cannot be established within the available window, including no action, partial activity, or invalid inputs.

We also report **non-exclusive action participation rates** based on whether a pair has positive recorded filled, cancelled, or modified quantity. These measure observed activity, not the final resolution of the entire displayed quote, and can overlap within a pair.

The categories add to 828. Of these, **342 pairs (41.30%)** receive a specified order-action classification. The remaining 486 are explicitly retained as unclassified rather than being treated as cancellations or non-fills.

### 3.2 Observed action participation (non-exclusive)

| Order action observed | Pairs with positive quantity | Share of 828 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Any fill | 16 | 1.93% |

These percentages **must not be summed**. For example, a pair with partial fills and cancellations may appear in both participation rows while having a `mixed` or `unclassified` final label. The 5 `filled` pairs in the previous table are therefore **not** the total number of pairs in which any fill was observed (16).

## 4. Unclassified observations and coverage

All 18 target instruments have MBO records in the analyzed sample; `no_mbo = 0`. The 486 unclassified outcomes are:

| Reason | Pairs | Share of all 828 |
|---|---:|---:|
| `no_action_before_end` | 289 | 34.90% |
| `partial_activity_only` | 196 | 23.67% |
| `invalid_window_or_price` | 1 | 0.12% |
| **Total** | **486** | **58.70%** |

The run reports zero observations flagged as `book_initialized == False`. This reflects the implementation's initialization flag and should **not** by itself be taken as independent proof that every resting order was recovered. Short and censored observation windows are an important reason why some complete quote-level outcomes cannot be observed. In particular, a partial removal or an absence of actions before cutoff is not evidence that the displayed quote was fully cancelled or filled.

## 5. Post-fill markouts

For each observed resting-order fill, we compare the fill price to the first available option midpoint at or after **1 ms, 10 ms, and 100 ms** after execution. The markout is signed from the **aggressive taker's perspective**:

\[
\mathrm{Markout}_{h}=s\bigl(\mathrm{Mid}_{t_{fill}+h}-P_{fill}\bigr),\quad
s=+1\text{ for buying a stale ask},\quad s=-1\text{ for selling a stale bid}.
\]

Dollar markouts use the $50 option-point multiplier. A positive value indicates a favorable subsequent midpoint relative to the taker's execution price.

| Horizon | Valid fill observations | Mean markout | Median markout | Positive markout share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| +1 ms | 19 | +$6.91 | +$2.50 | 57.89% |
| +10 ms | 19 | +$5.59 | $0.00 | 42.11% |
| +100 ms | 19 | +$2.63 | +$2.50 | 52.63% |

There are **19 distinct recorded fill observations** with valid markouts at each horizon, associated with **16 stale event–option pairs reporting at least one fill**. The 57 rows underlying this summary correspond to 19 fills × 3 horizons, not 57 independent executions. These estimates are exploratory because the fill sample is small. A midpoint markout is **not realized net trading P&L**; it does not establish an achievable exit price or account for the full costs of a round trip.

## 6. Interpretation and limitations

In this sample, changes to resting orders were recorded more often than outright fills. We observed a fill in 1.93% of stale pairs, a cancellation in 26.09%, and a modification in 51.81%; those are participation measures rather than mutually exclusive terminal outcomes. The high unclassified share (58.70%) is mainly associated with no observed action before cutoff or only partial activity. We should not interpret this as evidence that the remaining orders were cancelled, nor infer market-maker intent from the historical order messages.

Several limitations matter for interpretation:

1. **Timing and attribution.** Futures and options messages originate from different matching engines. The ordering of actions very close to the futures event timestamp may be sensitive to clock comparability. Issue #1 identified this as a constraint.
2. **Book reconstruction.** Reliable resting-order attribution depends on complete MBO history or initialization. A reported initialization flag does not rule out every missing or mis-reconstructed order; event-level `F`/`C` associations also merit inspection against real sequence/flag semantics.
3. **Observation-window censoring.** The Issue #4/#6 primary cutoff ends at the next raw futures midpoint change or another configured boundary, sometimes before the quote reaches an observable terminal state. A partial action is not necessarily the resolution of the whole top-of-book quote.
4. **No participant identity.** Order IDs and message actions do not reveal who owns the orders or why they were cancelled, filled, or modified.
5. **Limited external validity.** One 30-minute session on one day and 19 observed fills cannot support a broad claim about the full trading day, other dates, or a profitable execution strategy.

## 7. Reproducibility and outputs

The implementation keeps order reconstruction and classification in `src/stale_quotes/mbo_analysis.py`, with a script entry point in `scripts/run_mbo_analysis.py` and synthetic tests in `tests/test_mbo_analysis.py`. The final run uses the Issue #6 stale table and the Issue #4 baseline event table for October 7:

```bash
python scripts/run_mbo_analysis.py \
  --repo-root . \
  --date 2026-10-07 \
  --start-et 10:00 \
  --end-et 10:30 \
  --events data/processed/events/20261007/events.parquet
```

Small reviewable outputs are kept in `issue8_report/`:

- `resolution_summary_20261007.csv`
- `resolution_by_move_20261007.csv`
- `markout_summary_20261007.csv`
- `data_quality_20261007.csv`
- `summary.md` (this report)

Detailed `quote_resolutions.parquet`, `order_outcomes.parquet`, and `fill_markouts.parquet` remain in `data/processed/mbo/20261007/`, and raw MBO batches remain in `data/raw/mbo/20261007/`. These large local files are not intended for Git commits. The unclassified reason breakdown and action participation rates above were also computed directly from the local `quote_resolutions.parquet` and should be reproducible from the same input and code version.

**Bottom line:** The sample demonstrates a working order-level audit with explicitly reported cancellation, fill, unclassified rates and post-fill markouts. Given Issue #8's stretch scope and the size of MBO data, a broader download is not necessary for this deliverable. The evidence remains a small-sample robustness check, not a definitive estimate of how all stale quotes resolve.
